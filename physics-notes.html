Part I

Antiquity and the Long Middle Ages

Before anyone asked why the planets move, scribes recorded how they move.

Ch. 1

~750 BCE
Babylon & Egypt

Before Theory: Babylon, Egypt, and the Birth of Exact Astronomy

Physics did not begin with falling bodies; it began with the sky. The heavens offered the only natural phenomena that were simultaneously regular, quantifiable, and economically important. Long before anyone asked why the planets move, scribes recorded how they move.

Mesopotamia

From roughly the 8th century BCE, Babylonian temple scribes kept the Astronomical Diaries — nightly records sustained for some seven centuries, arguably the longest continuous scientific research program in history. Out of this data they built a sexagesimal (base-60) place-value arithmetic, predictive arithmetic schemes that modeled the varying speed of the Sun and Moon, and the Saros cycle (~18 years, 11⅓ days) extracted purely from records.

The crucial point: Babylonian astronomy was arithmetical and predictive, not geometrical or explanatory. There is no evidence they asked what the planets are or why they move. Physics required marrying this predictive ambition to a new Greek habit: explanation.

Egypt contributed practical geometry, a 365-day civil calendar, and timekeeping technology — but no theoretical astronomy comparable to Babylon's. What was missing everywhere: the idea that nature is governed by impersonal, universal, discoverable laws.

Ch. 2

6th century BCE
Ionian naturalism

The Greek Invention of Natural Philosophy

In the trading cities of Ionia, a handful of thinkers made the single most consequential move in the history of science: they proposed that the world can be explained naturalistically — by the properties of matter itself, without mythological actors.

Thales of Miletus (fl. ~585 BCE) proposed natural causes for earthquakes. Anaximander argued the earth floats unsupported at the center of the cosmos, held by symmetry — the first known argument from sufficient reason in cosmology. The Pythagorean school discovered that musical consonances correspond to simple whole-number ratios: the first known physical law expressed mathematically, seeding the conviction that mathematics is the language of nature.

Parmenides and Zeno produced the famous paradoxes of motion that forced two millennia of serious thought about continuity, infinity, and instantaneous velocity — issues only fully resolved by the calculus. The atomists (Leucippus, Democritus) answered: reality consists of unchanging atoms moving in the void; all apparent change is rearrangement.

Ch. 3

384–322 BCE
Aristotle

Aristotle's Physics: The System That Had to Be Overthrown

Aristotle built the most comprehensive, coherent, and empirically motivated physics of antiquity. It dominated for ~1,900 years — not because people were stupid, but because it explains everyday experience remarkably well. Understanding it is essential: Galileo and Newton are unintelligible except as its demolition.

His two-realm cosmos divided the sublunar realm (four elements, natural places, rectilinear motion) from the celestial realm (aether, eternal uniform circular motion). His theory of motion stated that speed of fall is proportional to weight and inversely proportional to the resistance of the medium — therefore a vacuum is impossible.

In a world of friction and air resistance, motion does die out unless maintained; feathers do fall slower than stones. Aristotle's physics is an excellent phenomenology of the terrestrial, dissipative world. Its fatal flaws: it is qualitative, teleological, and hard-wires a distinction between heaven and earth that forbids a universal mechanics.

Ch. 4

~300 BCE–170 CE
Hellenistic science

Hellenistic Exact Science

After Alexander, Greek science professionalized around the Museum and Library of Alexandria. Euclid's Elements codified geometry as a deductive system — the template of exact science. Archimedes founded both statics and hydrostatics as axiomatic mathematics, and developed proto-integration to compute curved areas and volumes.

Eratosthenes measures the Earth (~240 BCE). At summer solstice noon, the Sun is overhead at Syene (no shadow in a deep well) but casts a shadow at Alexandria corresponding to an angle \(\theta \approx 7.2° = 360°/50\) from the vertical. Since the Sun's rays are parallel, \(\theta\) equals the difference in latitude — the arc Syene–Alexandria is 1/50 of the full meridian circle. With the caravan-measured distance \(d \approx 5{,}000\) stadia: \[C = 50\,d \approx 250{,}000\ \text{stadia.}\] The entire measurement uses one theorem (alternate angles at parallel lines) and one assumption (a spherical Earth).

Aristarchus proposed a full heliocentric system 1,800 years before Copernicus. Hipparchus discovered the precession of the equinoxes and founded trigonometry. Ptolemy's Almagest synthesized everything into a predictive geocentric system that reigned for 1,400 years — a lesson that a theory can be predictively excellent and physically false.

Ch. 5

8th–15th century
Islamic & medieval

The Islamic Golden Age and the Medieval Latin West

Ibn al-Haytham's Book of Optics (~1021) is a strong candidate for the first work of recognizably modern experimental physics: he settled that we see by light entering the eye, performed systematic experiments with the camera obscura, and explicitly demanded that hypotheses be tested by controlled, repeatable experiment.

The Merton College Oxford Calculators (1320s–40s) treated motion for the first time as a quantity subject to theorems, proving the mean-speed theorem: a uniformly accelerated body covers the same distance as one moving uniformly at the mean of initial and final speeds.

Mean-speed theorem (Oresme's picture). Plot velocity against time. Uniform acceleration from \(v_0\) to \(v_f\) is a straight ramp; the distance traveled is the area under it — a trapezoid of average height \((v_0+v_f)/2\) and base \(t\). Algebraically: \[s = \int_0^t (v_0 + at')\,dt' = v_0 t + \tfrac{1}{2}at^2 = \frac{v_0+v_f}{2}\,t.\] Oresme's insight that distance = area under the velocity graph is the geometric core of integral calculus, three centuries early.

Galileo's law of fall is the Merton theorem applied to nature. The theorem itself was proved 300 years earlier.

Part II

The Scientific Revolution (1543–1687)

The decision that theory must bend to data, not data to theory — a hinge of scientific history.

Ch. 6

1543
Copernicus

Copernicus and the Heliocentric Hypothesis

Nicolaus Copernicus (1473–1543) published De revolutionibus orbium coelestium (1543). Placing the Sun near the center explains what Ptolemy merely fitted: retrograde motion becomes automatic at opposition; bounded elongation of Mercury and Venus is geometrically necessary; the order and scale of the system are fixed for the first time; planetary periods increase monotonically with distance.

What it did not do: Copernicus kept uniform circular motion and epicycles, and achieved accuracy only comparable to Ptolemy. The physical objections stood unanswered. The book converted few astronomers immediately; its indirect effects — via Kepler and Galileo — changed everything.

Ch. 7

1609–1619
Kepler's laws

Tycho's Data and Kepler's Laws

Tycho Brahe (1546–1601) pushed naked-eye positional accuracy to ~1–2 arcminutes, roughly ten times better than tradition. Two observations shattered celestial immutability: the new star of 1572 (a supernova) and the comet of 1577, both superlunary, the latter moving through the supposed crystalline spheres — which therefore do not exist.

Kepler's best circular model for Mars matched Tycho to within 8 arcminutes — and Kepler threw it away, because Tycho's data were good to 2. Out of the wreckage came his three laws:

  • First Law: each planet moves on an ellipse with the Sun at one focus. Two thousand years of circles end here.
  • Second Law: the Sun–planet line sweeps equal areas in equal times (conservation of angular momentum).
  • Third Law: \(T^2/a^3 = \text{const}\) — the fingerprint of an inverse-square force.
Ch. 8

1564–1642
Galileo

Galileo: Kinematics, the Telescope, and Relativity

Galileo argued by thought-experiment that all bodies fall alike, absent air resistance. The Merton theorem realized in nature: \(s \propto t^2\), \(v \propto t\).

The odd-number rule \(\Leftrightarrow\) \(s \propto t^2\). If \(s = \tfrac{1}{2}gt^2\), total distances after 1, 2, 3, 4,… equal time-units are proportional to 1, 4, 9, 16,…; distances in each successive unit are the differences 1, 3, 5, 7,… — the odd numbers. The mean-speed theorem then gives \(v_f = 2s/t = gt\), and eliminating \(t\): \(v^2 = 2gs\) — speed depends only on drop height.

The parabola. Horizontal speed \(v\) (unchanged, by inertia), vertical fall by the law of fall: \[x = vt,\quad y = \tfrac{1}{2}gt^2 \implies y = \frac{g}{2v^2}\,x^2,\] a parabola. Two already-established laws, superposed at right angles, yield a new exact result.

The telescope (1609–10) revealed lunar mountains, Jupiter's moons, the phases of Venus (geometrically impossible in Ptolemy), and sunspots. The ship argument in the Dialogue (1632) established that no mechanical experiment can detect uniform motion — dissolving every Aristotelian objection to a moving Earth. "The book of nature is written in the language of mathematics."

Ch. 9–10

1644–1704
Descartes, Huygens, Newton

Descartes, Huygens, and Newton

Descartes (1596–1650) stated the law of inertia cleanly, introduced analytic geometry (the indispensable language of all subsequent physics), and proposed vortex cosmology — wrong mechanism, but it made heliocentrism mechanically thinkable. Huygens (1629–1695) correctly solved elastic collisions with both momentum and vis viva conserved, quantified circular motion as \(a = v^2/r\), and built the pendulum clock.

Centripetal acceleration \(a = v^2/r\) (Huygens). A body on a circle would leave along the tangent. After time \(t\), the gap between tangent and circle: \((r+\delta)^2 = r^2 + (vt)^2\), so \(\delta \approx v^2t^2/2r\) — a gap growing as \(\tfrac{1}{2}(\text{const})t^2\), the signature of uniform acceleration: \[a = \frac{v^2}{r}.\] Corollary: inserting Kepler III (\(T^2 = kr^3\)) into \(a = 4\pi^2 r/T^2\) gives \(a \propto 1/r^2\) — the inverse-square hunch.

Newton's Principia (1687): three laws of motion; universal gravitation \(F = Gm_1m_2/r^2\); the Moon test confirming inverse-square at cosmic scale; Kepler's laws as theorems. Aristotle's two realms are dead: one mathematics, one law, heaven and earth united. Newton refuses to supply a mechanism: "hypotheses non fingo." That discomfort is cured only by general relativity, where gravity ceases to be a force at all.

The Moon test. The Moon's distance \(r \approx 60\,R_E\), period \(T = 27.32\text{ d}\). Its centripetal acceleration: \[a_\text{Moon} = \frac{4\pi^2 r}{T^2} \approx 2.7\times10^{-3}\ \text{m/s}^2 \approx \frac{g}{3600} = \frac{g}{60^2}.\] At 60 times the distance, \(60^2\) times weaker. One law, heaven and earth.

Part III

Classical Physics Matures (1687–1865)

Electricity + magnetism + optics: the second great unification after Newton.

Ch. 11

1788–1835
Lagrange, Hamilton

Analytical Mechanics: Euler, Lagrange, Hamilton

Lagrange's Mécanique analytique (1788) — a book famously containing "no figures" — reformulates mechanics as pure analysis. Define the Lagrangian \(\mathcal{L} = T - V\) in any generalized coordinates. The true motion makes the action stationary:

Euler–Lagrange from \(\delta S = 0\). Perturb the path: \(q(t) \to q(t) + \varepsilon\eta(t)\) with \(\eta\) vanishing at the endpoints. To first order in \(\varepsilon\): \[\delta S = \int_{t_1}^{t_2}\!\left(\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial q} - \frac{d}{dt}\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial\dot q}\right)\eta\,dt = 0.\] For this to vanish for every admissible \(\eta\), the bracket must vanish: the Euler–Lagrange equation. For \(\mathcal{L} = \tfrac{1}{2}m\dot x^2 - V(x)\) it reads \(m\ddot x = -V'(x)\): Newton recovered as the stationarity of a single scalar.

Noether in one line. If \(\mathcal{L}\) does not depend on \(q_i\) (a cyclic coordinate), Euler–Lagrange reads \(\frac{d}{dt}\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial\dot q_i}=0\), so \(p_i \equiv \frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial\dot q_i} = \text{const}\). Symmetry under translation \(\Rightarrow\) momentum conserved; under rotation \(\Rightarrow\) angular momentum; under time translation \(\Rightarrow\) energy.

Hamilton (1834–35) recast mechanics with momenta \(p_i = \partial\mathcal{L}/\partial\dot q_i\) and the Hamiltonian \(H(q,p)\): first-order flow in phase space. This formalism becomes the substrate of statistical mechanics and, later, quantum theory.

Ch. 13–14

1824–1877
Thermodynamics

Heat, Engines, and Statistical Mechanics

Carnot (1824) found the maximum efficiency of any heat engine between two temperatures: \[\eta_\text{max} = 1 - \frac{T_C}{T_H}.\] Mayer, Joule, and Helmholtz (1842–47) established the equivalence of heat and work; caloric died. Clausius (1854–65) distilled the Second Law into entropy \(S\), with \(\Delta S_\text{universe} \geq 0\). Irreversibility — time's arrow — enters physics as law.

Maxwell (1860) introduced probability into fundamental physics with the speed distribution \(f(v)\,dv \propto v^2 e^{-mv^2/2k_BT}\,dv\). Boltzmann completed the program: \(S = k_B \ln W\) — entropy measures the number of microstates compatible with a macrostate. The Second Law is not an absolute mechanical law but overwhelming probability.

Ch. 16

1831–1879
Faraday, Maxwell

Faraday's Fields and Maxwell's Synthesis

Faraday (1791–1867) introduced the field concept: action propagates through the field, not instantaneously at a distance. His 1845 Faraday effect — a magnetic field rotates light's polarization — was the first hard evidence light and electromagnetism are linked.

Maxwell (1831–1879) translated Faraday's geometry into dynamics. The decisive stroke: consistency with charge conservation forces a displacement current \(\varepsilon_0\,\partial\mathbf{E}/\partial t\), so a changing electric field generates a magnetic field. The complete Maxwell equations in vacuum produce a wave equation with speed:

Why the displacement current is forced. Take the divergence of the pre-Maxwell Ampère law \(\nabla\times\mathbf{B} = \mu_0\mathbf{J}\). The left side is identically zero, so it demands \(\nabla\cdot\mathbf{J}=0\) always — but charge conservation says \(\nabla\cdot\mathbf{J} = -\partial\rho/\partial t\), nonzero whenever charge piles up. Adding Maxwell's term: \[\nabla\cdot\!\left(\mu_0\mathbf{J} + \mu_0\varepsilon_0\frac{\partial\mathbf{E}}{\partial t}\right) = \mu_0\!\left(\nabla\cdot\mathbf{J} + \frac{\partial\rho}{\partial t}\right) = 0.\] Consistency alone dictates the new physics.

The wave equation. In vacuum, take the curl of Faraday's law and substitute Ampère–Maxwell: \[\nabla^2\mathbf{E} = \mu_0\varepsilon_0\,\frac{\partial^2\mathbf{E}}{\partial t^2},\] with propagation speed \(c = 1/\sqrt{\mu_0\varepsilon_0} \approx 3\times10^8\ \text{m/s}\) — the measured speed of light. Light's speed emerges from tabletop measurements of charges and currents: this coincidence is the discovery.

Maxwell, 1865: "Light itself is an electromagnetic disturbance in the form of waves." Optics annexed by electromagnetism: the second great unification after Newton's. Then Maxwell died at 48, in the year Einstein was born.

Part IV

Crisis and Relativity (1865–1919)

The happiest thought of my life: a freely falling observer does not feel his own weight.

Ch. 17

1887
Michelson–Morley

The Luminiferous Ether and the Michelson–Morley Experiment

Maxwell's equations contain a fixed speed \(c\) — presumably the speed relative to the ether. Earth, orbiting at \(v \approx 30\ \text{km/s}\), should feel an "ether wind" of \(v/c \approx 10^{-4}\). Detecting it required second-order sensitivity: \((v/c)^2 \approx 10^{-8}\).

The expected fringe shift. Arm along the wind: \[t_\parallel = \frac{2Lc}{c^2-v^2} = \frac{2L}{c}\cdot\frac{1}{1-v^2/c^2}.\] Arm across: \[t_\perp = \frac{2L}{\sqrt{c^2-v^2}} = \frac{2L}{c}\cdot\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-v^2/c^2}}.\] Rotating 90° swaps the roles, doubling the effect; the fringe shift: \[\Delta N = \frac{2L}{\lambda}\cdot\frac{v^2}{c^2} \approx \frac{2(11\ \text{m})(10^{-8})}{5.5\times10^{-7}\ \text{m}} \approx 0.4\ \text{fringe},\] forty times the instrument's sensitivity. Observed: effectively zero.

Null. The Earth does not move through the ether — yet aberration says the ether is not dragged along with Earth either. Every mechanical picture of the ether now contradicts some experiment.

Ch. 19

1905
Special relativity

Special Relativity (1905)

Einstein (26 years old, Bern patent office). Starting point: not Michelson–Morley but an asymmetry of description — magnet-and-conductor induction gets two different classical explanations depending on which moves. Two postulates: (1) the laws of physics take the same form in all inertial frames; (2) light propagates at speed \(c\) independent of the source's motion, in every inertial frame.

Time dilation from a light clock. A clock ticks by bouncing light between mirrors separated by \(d\): rest-frame tick \(\Delta\tau = 2d/c\). Watched from a frame where the clock moves at \(v\), the light travels the hypotenuse of a triangle: \[\left(\frac{c\Delta t}{2}\right)^2 = d^2 + \left(\frac{v\Delta t}{2}\right)^2 \implies \Delta t = \frac{2d/c}{\sqrt{1-v^2/c^2}} = \gamma\,\Delta\tau.\] Nothing but Pythagoras and the second postulate.

Velocity addition. Differentiate the Lorentz transformation: \[u' = \frac{u-v}{1-uv/c^2}.\] Check: \(u=c\) gives \(u'=c\). And for light in water (\(u=c/n\)) composed with water speed \(v\), to first order: \(u \approx c/n + v(1-1/n^2)\) — Fresnel's drag coefficient, with no ether at all.

\(E = mc^2\): mass and energy are interconvertible measures of the same thing. Dead: absolute simultaneity, absolute space and time, the mechanical ether. Alive: Maxwell's equations (already Lorentz-invariant); Newtonian mechanics as the \(v\ll c\) limit.

Ch. 21

1907–1915
Road to GR

The Road to General Relativity (1907–1915)

Writing a review of relativity in 1907, Einstein had "the happiest thought of my life": a freely falling observer does not feel his own weight. Because all bodies fall identically (Galileo, verified to \(10^{-8}\)–\(10^{-9}\) by Eötvös), gravity can be locally transformed away by free fall; conversely, a uniformly accelerated cabin is locally indistinguishable from one in a gravitational field. Gravity and inertia are the same thing.

Gravitational redshift from the equivalence principle. Replace the gravitational field \(g\) by a rocket of height \(h\) accelerating at \(g\). A light pulse takes time \(t \approx h/c\) to reach the ceiling, during which the ceiling gains speed \(v = gh/c\). The Doppler redshift: \[\frac{\Delta\nu}{\nu} \approx -\frac{v}{c} = -\frac{gh}{c^2} = -\frac{\Delta\phi}{c^2}.\] By equivalence, the same shift occurs in a real gravitational field. Since frequency is a clock, lower clocks run slower: \(d\tau \approx (1+\phi/c^2)\,dt\). This is the Pound–Rebka effect and the GPS correction.

In November 1915, in four consecutive Thursday communications to the Prussian Academy, Einstein restored general covariance; computed Mercury's anomalous perihelion advance — 43″ per century, exactly the unexplained residual since 1859 — and on 25 November presented the final field equations. "For a few days, I was beside myself with joyous excitement."

Ch. 22

November 1915
The equations

General Relativity: The Theory Itself

Matter and energy curve spacetime; freely falling bodies follow the straightest possible paths — geodesics — of that curved spacetime; what we call gravity is this geometry. Wheeler's epigram: "Spacetime tells matter how to move; matter tells spacetime how to curve."

Why \(G_{\mu\nu}\) on the left. We need a tensor equation "curvature = source." The source is \(T_{\mu\nu}\): symmetric, 10 components, locally conserved (\(\nabla^\mu T_{\mu\nu}=0\)). The left side must therefore be (i) a symmetric 2-tensor built from \(g\) and at most its second derivatives, and (ii) identically divergence-free. The contracted Bianchi identities supply exactly one such object: \[\nabla^\mu\!\left(R_{\mu\nu} - \tfrac{1}{2}R\,g_{\mu\nu}\right) \equiv 0.\] This is the mathematical fact that selects the Einstein tensor. Fixing the constant by matching the Newtonian weak-field limit: \[\boxed{G_{\mu\nu} = \frac{8\pi G}{c^4}\,T_{\mu\nu}.}\]

Schwarzschild's perihelion formula. From the geodesic equation, the Newtonian orbit equation in \(u=1/r\) gains one term: \[\frac{d^2u}{d\theta^2}+u = \frac{GM}{h^2}+\frac{3GM}{c^2}u^2.\] Treating the relativistic term perturbatively, the perihelion advances per orbit by \[\Delta\varphi = \frac{6\pi GM}{c^2 a(1-e^2)}.\] For Mercury (\(GM_\odot = 1.327\times10^{20}\ \text{m}^3/\text{s}^2\), \(a=5.79\times10^{10}\ \text{m}\), \(e=0.206\)): \(\Delta\varphi \approx 5.0\times10^{-7}\ \text{rad/orbit}\); at 415 orbits per century, \(\approx 43''\) — Le Verrier's orphaned residual, exactly.

Ch. 23

1915–1929
Classic tests

The Classic Tests and Relativistic Cosmology

  • Mercury's perihelion (1915): 43″/century, no free parameters. The failed planet Vulcan is exorcised.
  • Light deflection (1919): The full theory predicts 1.75″ at the solar limb — twice the Newtonian value. Eddington's eclipse expeditions to Sobral and Príncipe confirmed Einstein. "Revolution in Science… Newtonian Ideas Overthrown" (The Times, 7 Nov 1919).
  • Gravitational redshift (1960): Pound–Rebka confirmed \(gh/c^2 \approx 2.5\times10^{-15}\) over 22.5 m. Today, GPS satellite clocks must correct for both SR slowing (~−7 μs/day) and gravitational speeding (~+45 μs/day): relativity as daily engineering.

Applying the field equations to the universe, Einstein (1917) found that matter-filled universes collapse or expand — a static cosmos resists. Friedmann (1922), Lemaître (1927), and Hubble (1929) established the expanding universe. The same equations of November 1915 contain, as special cases: the falling apple, Mercury's orbit, bent starlight, slowed clocks, black holes, gravitational waves, and the expanding universe.

App. A

Mathematical toolkit

Minimal Mathematical Toolkit

The metric \(g_{\mu\nu}\) gives infinitesimal distance: \(ds^2 = g_{\mu\nu}dx^\mu dx^\nu\) (Einstein's summation convention: repeated indices summed — his own labor-saving invention, 1916). The connection \(\Gamma^\mu_{\alpha\beta} = \tfrac{1}{2}g^{\mu\nu}(\partial_\alpha g_{\nu\beta}+\partial_\beta g_{\nu\alpha}-\partial_\nu g_{\alpha\beta})\) defines parallel transport and covariant differentiation \(\nabla_\mu\). Curvature = non-commutativity of transport: \([\nabla_\mu,\nabla_\nu]V^\rho = R^\rho{}_{\sigma\mu\nu}V^\sigma\). Contractions: \(R_{\mu\nu} = R^\alpha{}_{\mu\alpha\nu}\), \(R = g^{\mu\nu}R_{\mu\nu}\). Bianchi identity \(\Rightarrow\) \(\nabla^\mu(R_{\mu\nu}-\tfrac{1}{2}Rg_{\mu\nu})=0\) — which is why exactly this combination can equal the conserved \(T_{\mu\nu}\).

Orders of magnitude worth memorizing: \(c = 2.998\times10^8\ \text{m/s}\); \(G = 6.674\times10^{-11}\ \text{N m}^2/\text{kg}^2\); Earth orbital speed 30 km/s (\(v/c = 10^{-4}\)); solar Schwarzschild radius 2.95 km; solar light deflection 1.75″; Mercury anomaly 43″/century; GPS relativistic correction ~38 μs/day net.
